SMART INDORE AI GRIEVANCE PORTALProduct Requirements Document01 · Product OverviewVision, problem statement, goals, users, and scope for the CivicLens platformDOCUMENT 1 OF 4 — PRODUCT OVERVIEWProject Codename: CivicLensScope: Pilot City — Indore, Madhya PradeshStatus: Draft v1.0Date: August 20261. Executive SummaryCivicLens (working title: Smart Indore AI Grievance Portal) is an AI-powered civic grievance redressal platform that converts citizen-reported problems into structured, prioritized, and trackable public infrastructure actions for the Indore Municipal Corporation (IMC). The platform pairs a citizen-facing complaint app with an AI-assisted government operations dashboard, using computer vision, duplicate detection, location intelligence, and multilingual AI classification to route complaints to the correct department and estimate resolution timelines.The pilot is scoped to Indore city, with an architecture designed to generalize to additional Urban Local Bodies (ULBs) in future phases.2. Problem StatementCitizens currently report civic issues (potholes, drainage, garbage, streetlights, water leakage, etc.) through fragmented, low-visibility channels — phone calls, in-person visits, or generic complaint portals — with little transparency into status or timelines.Government departments receive complaints without automatic categorization, priority ranking, or duplicate detection, leading to redundant site visits, delayed triage, and inconsistent SLA tracking.There is no unified, real-time view of complaint volume by category, location, or department workload, making it difficult for IMC administrators to allocate resources or measure department performance.Language and literacy barriers prevent many citizens from filing detailed complaints through text-only forms.Complaints that pose higher public-health or safety risk (e.g., open drains, electrical hazards) are not distinguished from low-urgency issues, so they compete equally for attention.3. Vision StatementTo make every civic complaint in Indore visible, verifiable, and accountable — using AI to compress the time between a citizen noticing a problem and the right municipal department acting on it, while giving IMC leadership a live, data-driven view of city-wide infrastructure health.4. Goals &amp; Objectives4.1 Business / Municipal GoalsReduce average complaint resolution time across all IMC departments.Increase citizen trust and reporting participation through visible, two-way status tracking.Give IMC leadership real-time, evidence-based visibility into department workload, SLA compliance, and recurring problem hotspots.Reduce duplicate site visits and wasted field-staff time via automated duplicate complaint detection.Prioritize complaints with genuine public-health or safety risk over routine maintenance requests.4.2 Product GoalsShip a functioning pilot for Indore covering complaint submission, AI categorization/routing, and two role-based dashboards (Citizen, Government).Support voice, image, and text complaint submission in multiple languages.Provide government officers an AI &quot;copilot&quot; that recommends priority, department, and estimated resolution time for every incoming complaint.Provide analytics covering complaints by category, complaints by location, department workload, resolution time, critical complaints, pending complaints, and SLA violations.5. Target Users &amp; Personas5.1 Citizen (Primary Reporter)Who: Resident of Indore reporting a civic issue (road damage, garbage, water leakage, drainage, electrical fault, etc.)Needs: A fast, low-effort way to report an issue (voice/photo/text) in their preferred language, and clear visibility into what happens nextPain points: Not knowing which department to contact, no confirmation their complaint was received, no timeline, no resolution proof5.2 Government Officer / Department StaffWho: IMC department staff who triage, assign, and resolve complaints (e.g., Public Works, Health/Sanitation, Water Works)Needs: A prioritized queue of complaints relevant to their department, AI-suggested priority and category, tools to accept/reject and update statusPain points: Manual triage, no duplicate detection, unclear complaint urgency, no structured SLA tracking5.3 IMC Administrator / LeadershipWho: City-level administrators overseeing department performance and city infrastructure healthNeeds: Aggregate dashboards: complaints by category/location, department workload, resolution time, SLA violations, critical/pending countsPain points: No cross-department visibility, no data to justify resourcing decisions6. Scope6.1 In Scope (Pilot — Indore City)Citizen dashboard: registration/login, location detection or manual city/area selection, complaint submission via voice, image, or text, multilingual input.AI categorization: priority classification (factoring population density and public-health/safety severity) and department routing across all 13 IMC departments and named sub-branches.Duplicate complaint detection using location + image/content similarity.Government dashboard: complaint queue, accept/reject with estimated resolution time, status updates, department workload and SLA analytics.Citizen-facing status tracking: submitted → accepted/rejected → in progress → resolved, with notifications.Map-based location intelligence using Leaflet + OpenStreetMap.Supabase-backed auth, database, and file storage (for complaint images).6.2 Out of Scope (Pilot Phase)Multi-city / multi-ULB support (architected for it, not enabled in pilot).Native mobile apps (pilot is web-first, responsive).Payment or penalty processing.Field-staff mobile work-order app (officers use the web dashboard only).Public open-data API for third parties.7. Assumptions &amp; ConstraintsPilot is limited to Indore city boundaries; location intelligence and department mapping are Indore-specific for v1.Citizens have access to a smartphone or computer with camera/microphone for image and voice complaint submission.IMC will provide or approve the authoritative list of departments, sub-branches, and SLA targets used for routing and analytics.AI classification quality depends on model access via OpenRouter; a human-in-the-loop (officer review) step is required for the pilot rather than fully autonomous routing.Supabase is the assumed backend-as-a-service for auth, Postgres database, and file storage for the pilot.10. Release ApproachThe pilot follows a phased approach: (1) core complaint submission + manual/AI-assisted routing, (2) government dashboard with analytics, (3) duplicate detection and AI Grievance Copilot maturation, (4) multilingual and voice input hardening. Each phase is validated against the KPIs in Section 7 before broadening city coverage.
